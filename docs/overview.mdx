Every switch vendor reports telemetry its own way. Cisco calls a counter
`in-discards-64`, Nokia's SR Linux calls the same thing
`statistics/in-discarded-packets`, and the units, nesting and enums differ too.
A monitoring tool has to learn each vendor's names before it can show anything,
and learn them again whenever a schema changes.

We split the work into three products, each in its own repository:

| Product | What it does |
|---|---|
| [Telemetry pipeline](./pipeline.mdx) | Collects telemetry from switches, translates it into [OpenConfig](https://openconfig.net/) and publishes it on a broker. |
| [Observability platform](./platform.mdx) | One subscriber of the pipeline: a dashboard, history and device control for network operators. |
| [SONiC lab](./sonic-lab.mdx) | Simulated SONiC switches that stand in for real hardware. |

## Two paths through the system

<Flow />

Every message takes the top path, and none of it involves an LLM. A mapping
turns one vendor's schema into OpenConfig, and once it's approved, translating
a record is a lookup. The bottom path runs only when the pipeline meets a schema
it has no mapping for. A small local model proposes a mapping, and a person
reviews it. Records that arrive in the meantime wait, and they're replayed once
the mapping is approved.

## Where they meet

The products share no code. They meet in only three places:

- **The broker.** The pipeline publishes on Kafka topics, and the normalized
  topic is its public API. Its version is in the topic name and in every
  message, and a breaking change gets a new topic, so a subscriber is never
  broken in place.
- **One HTTP API.** The platform tells the pipeline which switches to collect
  from through the pipeline's targets API, the way any other client would.
- **The devices.** Each product reaches a switch on its own, over SSH with its
  own key. gNMI comes next, for switches that support it.

Because those are the only seams, replacing the lab with real switches changes
nothing in the pipeline or the platform, and the platform is one subscriber
among any number.

## Who owns what

- **The pipeline** owns the list of switches to collect from, the broker and
  its topics, and the mappings, with their review.
- **The platform** owns the device inventory and everything that changes a
  device: snapshots, drift, heal and restore.
- **The lab** creates switches and nothing more. Onboarding, collecting from
  and repairing them is the other two products' job, done the same way it
  would be for real hardware.
